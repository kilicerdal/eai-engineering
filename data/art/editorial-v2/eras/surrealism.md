# Making recognition uncertain

> Surrealism gave dreams, desire and irrational associations a public artistic program, while individual painters devised sharply different procedures for unsettling recognition.

## Beyond the illustration of a dream | analysis | 1

A surprising picture is not automatically a record of a dream. The painter constructs an encounter through choices of scale, finish, juxtaposition and naming. Dalí can make an object change its material behavior while surrounding space remains persuasive. Magritte can keep an object recognizable while changing its relationship to a word. These procedures deserve separate descriptions before being gathered under a movement label.

## The authority of an ordinary surface | analysis | 1

Clear depiction is particularly powerful when it promises an explanation and then frustrates it. A cast shadow, a familiar silhouette or a grammatical sentence ordinarily makes a visual demonstration seem trustworthy. A painting can preserve those devices while making their promise unreliable. The result is not necessarily illegibility. Often it is a precise uncertainty about what a perfectly legible image allows the viewer to conclude.

## Interpret without supplying a universal code | analysis | 1

Avoid a dictionary in which an eye, an egg or a pipe always means the same hidden thing. A proposed interpretation should explain a particular arrangement and acknowledge what it does not settle. An artist’s statement supplies a historical voice with its own purpose and timing; it does not cancel every alternative reading. Begin with the visible relation, then ask how the surrounding title, text or historical setting changes its force.
