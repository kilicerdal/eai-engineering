## Many centers, different ambitions | history | 1,2
Florence, Rome, Milan and other centers offered different conditions for making art. Courts, religious communities, civic institutions and private households commissioned images for particular purposes. The renewed study of antiquity mattered, but it coexisted with Christian devotion and contemporary ambitions for status and memory.

The artists represented here do not supply a complete account of the period. Their presence reflects a selective collection. Leonardo, Michelangelo and Raphael nevertheless demonstrate how different solutions could emerge from overlapping artistic environments.
## Representation as an organized argument | analysis |
Perspective could give a scene a coherent spatial order, but it was never only a mathematical achievement. In Raphael’s School of Athens, architecture distributes intellectual authority. In Leonardo’s Last Supper, converging space intensifies a narrative of recognition and reaction.

Michelangelo gives the body a different role. The Sistine figures make anatomy carry theological and emotional pressure on an architectural scale. Comparing these painters reveals alternatives within the period rather than one shared formula for excellence.
## The workshop and the commission | history | 1,2
Large artistic projects required resources, collaboration and negotiation. Training involved practical work as well as study; patrons shaped the scale, location and expectations of an image. An artist’s reputation could grow through major commissions, but those commissions also imposed constraints.

Remembering these conditions makes the paintings more intelligible. A refectory wall, a chapel ceiling and a private portrait panel invite different kinds of looking. The original audience and position belong to the meaning of the work, even when a digital gallery places them side by side.
## What to compare | interpretation |
Follow the ways a gesture moves attention through a group. Ask whether architecture stabilizes the scene or whether a body supplies its main structure. Notice where an image invites physical closeness and where scale establishes authority.

The Renaissance is most useful here as a field of problems and possibilities. Its painters did not simply make the world look more real. They developed persuasive ways of connecting visible forms to religious, social and intellectual claims.
