# Modernism is a disagreement, not a destination

> Painting’s means of description become subjects of investigation alongside the changing conditions of modern life. A broad category connects the works without making their differences disappear.

## Changing the terms of representation | analysis | 1

Modernism is useful as a framework for asking how inherited conventions were tested. It becomes misleading when it turns into a single inevitable progression from realism to abstraction. Picasso’s figures, Matisse’s colored spaces and Kahlo’s self-representations do not solve the same problem in the same way. Their differences are the reason to put them into conversation rather than to arrange them as steps toward a final answer.

## Public life and the construction of an image | analysis | 1

Patronage, collecting, exhibition and the professional opportunities available to different artists shape that conversation. A studio can be represented as an account of artistic identity; a self-portrait can stage the social meanings of dress. These are not simply private experiments sealed away from history. Nor can a museum’s later recognition be treated as proof that a painting met with immediate understanding at the moment of its making.

## Three questions to carry into the gallery | analysis | 1

Ask first what relation the painting changes: between figure and ground, between color and description, or between a person and the public signs through which identity becomes visible. Then ask what evidence would support a historical explanation of that change. Finally, examine what the broad category leaves out. Mexican modernism, for example, requires its own institutional and political histories, not merely an assigned place within a story told from Paris.
