## Restrictions were not identical | history | 1,2,3
These artists lived in different centuries and worked within different institutions. Gentileschi’s training and international commissions cannot be explained through the same circumstances as Morisot’s participation in independent exhibitions or Cassatt’s career in France. Gender matters, but it does not make their histories interchangeable.

A serious account asks which routes to training, models, patrons and public recognition were available in each case. It also recognizes the artists’ choices within those circumstances.
## Subjects are not measures of ambition | analysis |
Gentileschi’s Judith makes bodily agency depend on coordinated force. Morisot’s Cradle organizes an intimate space through a veil and a concentrated gaze. Cassatt’s Child’s Bath makes compressed perspective and pattern carry the act of care. The scale of the depicted event does not determine the sophistication of the painting.

Domestic subjects should not be praised only after being declared exceptions to domesticity. Their artistic complexity is already visible in the construction of space, attention and relation.
## Beyond recovery as a slogan | interpretation |
Including women in a collection is a beginning rather than a sufficient interpretation. Their work deserves the same specificity of analysis given to any major painter. Biography should explain circumstances without turning paintings into automatic illustrations of oppression or personal trauma.

The most valuable comparison preserves difference. These careers demonstrate several ways of establishing artistic authority, and their paintings expand what viewers can recognize as a consequential subject.
