# How to discuss abstraction with precision
> An abstract painting does not need a hidden object to be intelligible. Its relationships can be described, tested and compared directly.

## Begin with what the image makes visible | analysis |
Identify a relationship rather than guessing a subject. A large dark form can anchor one corner; a diagonal can interrupt a horizontal rhythm; an overlap can suggest depth while leaving the surrounding field flat. These statements can be checked against the picture.

Change distance. Some structures become clearer when individual strokes recede, while other effects depend on close attention to material handling. Neither position is automatically the correct one.
## Context gives the choices consequence | history | 1,2
Kandinsky’s theories and Bauhaus teaching supply a historical framework for his concern with color and form. His interest in music offered an analogy for a painting not dependent on conventional narrative. These ideas help explain his ambitions without proving universal rules about how every viewer must respond.

The paintings also differ from one another. Composition VII’s dense currents and Several Circles’ suspended geometry do not create the same experience simply because both are abstract.
## Interpretation follows an argument | interpretation |
A personal association can begin a reading, but it becomes stronger when connected to visible evidence. If a work feels restless, ask which directions conflict. If it feels calm, ask how its weights and intervals are held. Explain what changes when one form is considered beside another.

This approach preserves openness without accepting vagueness. The viewer does not have to discover a secret caption. They develop an account of how the painting works—and can revise that account as closer looking reveals new relationships.
