> Van Gogh’s short painting career was built through repeated study, correspondence and deliberate changes of method. The movement from rural supper to constructed night sky is a history of pictorial labor, not a license to diagnose every image through the dramatic events of his life.

## A late commitment to an artistic vocation | history | 1,4 | section-1

Born in the Netherlands in 1853, Vincent van Gogh passed through several occupations before committing himself to art. Work in the art trade and his engagement with religious life formed part of that search. He did not begin as the fully developed painter of brilliant color familiar from reproductions. Drawing, the study of figures and sustained attention to rural labor were central to his early development.

The Potato Eaters was an ambitious attempt to make a substantial figure composition. Its dark palette and emphatic hands belong to that purpose. The painting deserves to be read as a considered project, even where its forms are awkward, rather than as a primitive stage whose only value is to precede the later pictures.

## Paris, exchange and the transformation of color | history | 1,2 | section-2

In Paris, Van Gogh encountered modern French painting and artists experimenting with broken color and new subjects. Japanese prints also offered compositional possibilities. His relation to these sources was selective: color, contour and viewpoint became tools for an expressive practice rather than elements of a uniform borrowed style.

His brother Theo supplied financial and professional support. Their correspondence is an unusually rich source for the painter’s thinking, practical needs and ambitions. Letters must nevertheless be dated and connected to the correct work. A statement about one painting should not be moved to another simply because the images share a subject or atmosphere.

## Arles and the ambition of a shared artistic life | history | 1,2 | section-3

The move to Arles in 1888 brought a period of intense work and the hope of establishing a community of artists. The visit of Paul Gauguin formed part of that project, followed by conflict and a severe personal crisis. These events matter, but they do not explain every color or distortion in the paintings produced there.

The Bedroom is particularly revealing because expressive choices can be discussed alongside the practical idea of a room. The tilted space and simplified forms construct an image of domestic order that may feel less stable than the subject initially suggests. Its different versions also require separate dates and records; the Amsterdam painting shown here is not interchangeable with the later repetitions.

## A brushstroke is a decision | analysis |  | section-4

Van Gogh’s marks frequently carry direction. A stroke can follow the curvature of a hill, emphasize the growth of a cypress or make a field appear to move across the surface. Contour and color can hold an object apart from its surroundings even while repeated marks connect it to a larger rhythm. Expression emerges from these relations, not from the mere fact that the paint is visibly energetic.

The Night Café and Café Terrace at Night demonstrate different uses of color in related settings. The first concentrates an interior into an uneasy arrangement of furniture and light. The second opens an illuminated terrace toward a night sky. Similar subject matter does not mean identical emotional or compositional aims.

## Illness without the myth of effortless genius | interpretation | 1,3 | section-5

Van Gogh experienced serious episodes of illness and spent time in the asylum at Saint-Rémy. The Starry Night belongs to that period, but describing it as a direct record of a symptom is neither a sufficient interpretation nor a medical conclusion the painting can establish. Observed motifs, remembered places and compositional invention coexist within it.

Wheatfield with Crows likewise requires care. Its position among the final works does not establish it as the last painting, and its birds do not prove one intended message about death. The attraction of a dramatic narrative should not override the documented limits of chronology.

## An afterlife shaped by preservation and interpretation | history | 1,2 | section-6

Van Gogh died in 1890. The later circulation, publication and collection of his work helped build the extraordinary reputation it now carries. His artistic importance is better served by examining how he made images than by treating suffering as their necessary source. The collection offers a route from rural figure painting to the increasingly deliberate organization of color, space and mark—a sustained professional investigation carried out under difficult circumstances.

## A working intelligence behind the expressive surface | interpretation |  | section-7

The differences between the early figure paintings, the constructed interior and the Saint-Rémy landscape are fundamental. Changes in color and handling do not erase the continuing labor of organizing a world. The visible stroke can describe a surface, set a direction and establish a relation to another mark at the same time.

A professional biography must hold hardship and agency together. The circumstances of the artist’s life matter, but they do not turn artistic work into an effortless discharge of pain. Exchange, study, material decisions and revision remain visible in the paintings. The later reputation can also obscure the role of preservation and interpretation in bringing the work to wider audiences. The familiar image of an isolated genius is less informative than a career understood through its actual efforts and connections.

## Working circumstances and the evidence of pictures | analysis |  | section-8

The Potato Eaters, The Night Cafe and Wheatfield with Crows differ in the demands they place on color and direction. The career is a history of practice as well as biography. Letters can establish what the painter said at a particular time, but a selected excerpt should retain its context and cannot explain every later picture. Avoid the unsupported claim that a dramatic landscape must be a final work or that a directional brushstroke is a transparent symptom of illness.
