# A child who will not sit ceremonially
@reviewed 2026-11-20
@source {"title":"National Gallery of Art · Little Girl in a Blue Armchair","url":"https://www.nga.gov/artworks/61368-little-girl-blue-armchair","scope":"1878 work, sitter, dog and documented Degas participation"}

> Little Girl in a Blue Armchair sets a child’s expansive, informal body against a room whose elegant furniture seems designed for a different kind of behavior.

## A painting with a documented collaboration | history | 1 | section-1

The child was the daughter of friends of Degas. Cassatt later recorded that he had advised her on the background and worked on the painting. This evidence belongs to the work’s history without making Cassatt’s authorship disappear. The exact division of every brushstroke is a more specialized question than the general fact of participation.

The dog occupies another chair, reinforcing the impression that the room is being used rather than formally presented. Expensive furnishings establish a social setting, while the child’s posture refuses its expected ceremonial composure.

## Furniture becomes a field of pressure | analysis |  | section-2

The chairs’ blue upholstery occupies much of the canvas. Their repeated shapes and patterns surround a relatively small body whose sprawling pose asserts a different rhythm. The low, oblique viewpoint makes the room feel immediate and slightly disorienting.

The girl’s limbs do not close into a tidy portrait silhouette. Her body seems to test the space available to it. The sleeping dog provides a quieter parallel, another occupant whose behavior is not organized around the spectator’s approval.

## Childhood without miniature adulthood | interpretation |  | section-3

Boredom, fatigue or defiance are possible responses to the pose, but the picture does not establish one complete psychological story. Its achievement is to allow the child a bodily presence that does not conform to polished social display.

Cassatt makes a tension between environment and inhabitant visible. The room’s propriety remains, but the girl occupies it on her own terms. The result gives an informal moment the compositional force of a serious portrait while questioning what a portrait is expected to demand of a child.
