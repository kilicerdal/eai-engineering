# Art Room — reading and evidence pass

Local preview: http://127.0.0.1:3038/study/art

This pass updates the existing Art Room. It does not publish, introduce another service, replace artwork, or migrate personal storage. Earlier uncommitted depth-and-facts work is retained. “25 areas” below describes the approved review scope, not 25 new services or a claim that every paragraph was rewritten.

## Delivered changes

- Six modern painter biographies gain specific career circumstances, institutional references and individual section headings: Picasso, Dalí, Pollock, Matisse, Kahlo and Magritte. Original leads, paragraphs and section identifiers remain.
- Ten further painting studies gain concrete introductions and looking-based conclusions: Saturn, The Psyche Mirror, Young Mother Sewing, The Blue Rider, In the Dining Room, Dido Building Carthage, Irises, Still Life with Plaster Cupid, Women in the Garden and The Harbor at Lorient. Earlier additions to ten other shorter studies remain intact.
- Nineteen fact entries have revised wording and evidence notes. Two repeated object summaries become career discoveries: Caravaggio’s public commission and the Blue Rider association. Stable fact identifiers remain.
- Relevant painter facts link to their exact works, including the correct distinction between Kahlo’s Cropped Hair and The Two Fridas.
- Evidence explanations move into an optional disclosure. External source links announce their new-tab behavior; native return links retain the working reading-mode transition.
- All five existing visual comparisons now explain the perceptual consequence of the difference they identify. Images, dimensions, credits, licenses and source URLs remain.
- A numerical comparison register covers all 40 fact bodies containing numerals. It separates event, execution, exhibition, acquisition and title dates. It is available in the existing editorial review desk.
- Summer’s Day is dated **About 1879**, following the National Gallery record. The corresponding fact preserves the qualification and distinguishes the 1917 Lane credit.
