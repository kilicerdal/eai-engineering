# Art Room: Looking Room local review

2026-11-20. Local implementation only; no commit, push or deployment performed. Preview: http://127.0.0.1:3038/study/art/looking-room . Existing 112 paintings, 21 painters, original 100 reproduction records, 200 books, 12 certification paths, 1,090 questions and saved-work keys remain intact.

## Approved package and scope

1. Modern image permissions: added a clear image-access directory for all twelve modern works, direct institutional viewing and published licensing routes. Permission has not been obtained and no licensing request was sent. No unlicensed image files added.
2. Distinctive introductions: six modern painter leads now establish different artistic problems rather than repeat a movement formula.
3. Two-work arguments: six individual pairing essays explain what changes between each painter’s two studies.
4. Professional circumstances: six biographical additions address training, work, patronage, economic conditions and institutional mediation, with cited sources.
5. Movement labels: six explicit discussions identify what familiar classifications obscure.
6–7. Titles and scale: six object-specific cases distinguish naming history from meaning and explain physical scale without pretending a screen duplicates the object.
8. Composition: eight removable diagrams use original credited reproductions. Editorial paths are labelled approximate, not underdrawing or technical evidence.
9. Close details: three paired studies allow a selected crop to return to the whole painting; every view carries credit and reuse context.
10. Color: six relational readings identify themselves as visual analysis of reproductions, not pigment measurement.
11. Making: six bounded sequences distinguish documented stages from unknown order. The Red Studio follows published technical research; no universal reconstruction is fabricated.
12. Conservation: two documented decisions, Pollock’s One and Matisse’s Red Studio, separate evidence, treatment decisions and remaining limits.
13–14. Journey and reception: dated selected milestones and distinct reception moments in six cases; ownership gaps remain explicit.
15. Primary voices: a fourth short primary excerpt and named, attributed companion perspectives distinguish artist statements from later interpretation. Existing voices retained.
16–17. Interpretation: six cases explain what evidence might change a reading and test a familiar story against what the record supports.
18. Other people: case essays and painter additions discuss collaborators, patrons, dealers, partners and institutional actors where documented.
19. Cross-century comparisons: three visual pairs and four routes compare formal problems without claiming undocumented influence or inevitable progress.
20. Question-led reading: four resumable three-work routes, each with specific transitions, looking questions and a closing question.
21. Argument notebook: private interpretations, visible observations, references and reconsideration fields can attach a saved essay passage. Explicit Save, edit, remove, export and full-backup restore support are included.
22. Continuity: painting essay sections can be bookmarked independently of completion. Marking an essay read preserves the saved section. Old backups still parse; conflicting argument edits require an explicit restore choice.
23. Editorial history: four substantive before/after records explain dating, ownership and conservation corrections. Automated source maintenance flags are separate from scholarly approval.
24. Exhibition rhythm: the entrance retains three primary choices. The Looking Room groups deeper tools behind optional disclosures; four reading routes fit a restrained desktop grid and single mobile column.
