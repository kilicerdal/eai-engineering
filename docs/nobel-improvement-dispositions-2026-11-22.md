Local implementation record for the 50-item improvement list. This records what changed in the collection and where editorial verification is still required; it does not imply that every historical claim has been newly researched.

## Product changes in this pass

- Reading progress is calculated from the rendered article and saved in browser-local storage. The story page offers a section-level resume link; the collection offers a cross-visit continue-reading link. No account or server storage is involved.
- Readers can enlarge or reduce article text, switch to a focused reading layout, and print an individual story. These preferences persist in the same browser.
- The collection includes a field and award-decade coverage table. It describes the current 18-study selection and explicitly cautions against treating it as a measure of the complete award history.
- Existing story citations, source scope notes, review dates, evidence distinctions, contributor maps, timelines, reflection prompts, field navigation, paired stories and award-year labels remain in the reader.
- Existing mobile rules reflow the collection, tables, article controls and reading layout. Keyboard focus styling, landmarks, skip links, semantic headings and labelled controls remain in place.

## Editorial disposition of the 50 recommendations

The collection's existing content already contains question-led introductions, dated accounts, turning-point chapters, outcome/limit discussions, official award records, contributor roles, source-specific citations, uncertainty notes, discussion prompts, further reading, scope language, related-story links, and language distinguishing recognition from later application. The editorial standard in `nobel-editorial-standard.md` governs how those elements should be maintained.

The following require a story-by-story source review before they can honestly be called complete: a documented “before” baseline and surprise for every story; a sourced account of what changed afterward; claim-level distinction between direct documentation and interpretation; source perspective/limitations; collaborators and institutions beyond the existing profiles; mentorship and geographic/political context where material; credit omissions and shared attribution; field-specific gaps in selection; citation/date/terminology consistency; and renewed availability checks for links. These require checking each cited source and, where needed, adding reliable research. No new attribution or historical conclusion was invented in this pass.

## Editorial checklist for the remaining source pass

For each of the 18 studies, check the award record and each cited source, then record:

1. What readers could reasonably know before the work, and what remained unresolved.
2. The chronology of the work, its best-supported turning point, and the later consequence that can be attributed to it.
3. What the source directly documents, what the evidence supports, and what is interpretation.
4. Which collaborators, institutions, prior traditions, places or political conditions materially shaped the result.
5. How the award citation frames the achievement, who is named, and what the sources can establish about wider contribution.
6. The source's author/institution, scope, limitations, current link status, and last checked date.
7. Accurate use of “first,” “discovered,” “invented,” “proved,” “impact” and the formal prize name.
8. Whether the article's final section states a consequence or open question without overstating the award's reach.

Review the aggregate selection separately for era, region, language, discipline, institutional access and contribution type. Treat uneven counts as a prompt for editorial attention, not an automatic quota.
