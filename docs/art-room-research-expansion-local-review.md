## Research tools

1. Twenty signature works have optional object-specific dossiers covering materials, patronage, ownership, reception and unresolved questions. Missing provenance or technical evidence is named; complete chains of ownership are not fabricated.
2. Five visual comparisons show licensed/public-domain reproductions uncropped: Potato Eaters painting and lithograph; two Bedrooms; Night Watch and Lundens’s copy; two Rouen Cathedral canvases; and two Card Players versions. Individual credits and source records accompany each image. Five new optimized assets add approximately 577 KiB.
3. Open questions distinguish attributed published interpretations, unsettled documentary questions and catalogue discrepancies. The Vermeer and Leonardo discussions cite institutional scholarship. Magritte’s date discrepancy is an editorial flag, not an invented debate between historians.
4. Three short primary-voice passages carry source, date, context and limits: Van Gogh, Pollock and Monet. Artist intention is distinguished from proof about a specific painting.
5. Selected painting journeys distinguish securely identified institutional stages from gaps. They are not comprehensive provenance histories.
6. Optional, ungraded examples separate visible observation, documentary evidence and interpretation.
7. Scholarship search matches passages across painter, painting, era and thematic essays, plus dossier subjects. Results link to the relevant section and reveal a closed research disclosure when required.
8. A repeatable reference-health script records HTTP status and public text hashes for 37 selected references. Access restrictions are not labelled broken links. Changed text and revised attribution flags require human editorial review. This is not an audit of every reference or automated scholarly verification.
9. Museum preparation lets visitors choose up to six works, filter by recorded collection, and print looking questions with handwritten note space. Selection does not rewrite saved learning work. The guide makes no promise that a museum currently displays a work.
10. Collection notes explain selection, connections and major omitted traditions. New depth sits behind optional routes rather than crowding the entrance.
